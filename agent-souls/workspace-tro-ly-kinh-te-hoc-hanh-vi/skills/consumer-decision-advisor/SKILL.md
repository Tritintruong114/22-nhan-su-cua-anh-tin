# Consumer Decision Advisor

## Purpose
Use the structure of Melina Palmer's _What Your Customer Wants and Can't Tell You_ as a practical advisory framework for Vietnamese-first consulting on customer behavior, sales, pricing, marketing messages, and purchase decisions.

This skill is not a substitute for the full copyrighted book. It is a working synthesis from public metadata, legitimate summaries/previews, and behavioral economics knowledge.

## Source Structure To Preserve
The book is organized as three practical layers:

1. **Brain and business foundation**: why customers cannot always explain what they want, how the brain shortcuts decisions, and why business messaging must be brain-friendly.
2. **Core behavioral concepts**: framing, priming, anchoring and adjustment, relativity, loss aversion, scarcity, herding, social proof, nudges and choice architecture, paradox of choice, partitioning, pain of paying, surprise and delight, peak-end rule, habits, reciprocity.
3. **Application layer**: behavioral baking, pricing, selling more of the right stuff, small steps, ordering/conversion flow, problem definition, novelty and story, testing, and avoiding stuckness.

## Default Response Structure
When anh asks about a customer, product, offer, campaign, funnel, landing page, sales script, price, objection, or buying behavior, answer in Vietnamese and prefer this structure:

1. **Chan doan hanh vi**: what decision moment is happening?
2. **Co che tam ly**: which behavioral concepts explain it?
3. **Tin hieu khach hang**: what observable signals would confirm or disconfirm the diagnosis?
4. **Ung dung kinh doanh**: what to change in offer, price, message, proof, UX, sales flow, or follow-up?
5. **Cau hoi kiem chung**: what should anh ask customers or inspect in real data?
6. **Thu nghiem nho**: one low-risk A/B test or field experiment.

## Decision Map
Use this map before giving advice:

- **Attention stage**: framing, priming, novelty, salience.
- **Understanding stage**: relativity, anchoring, partitioning, paradox of choice.
- **Trust stage**: social proof, herding, reciprocity, brand cues.
- **Risk stage**: loss aversion, scarcity, guarantees, pain of paying.
- **Action stage**: nudges, choice architecture, default options, small steps.
- **Memory and loyalty stage**: surprise and delight, peak-end rule, habits.

## Concept Summaries And Consulting Use

### Framing
- Mechanism: customers react to how information is framed, not only to the information itself.
- Signals: same offer gets different reactions when worded differently; customers misunderstand value because the comparison frame is wrong.
- Application: rewrite claims around outcome, risk avoided, identity, or before-after contrast.
- Verification question: "Khach dang so sanh san pham nay voi cai gi trong dau?"

### Priming
- Mechanism: prior cues shape later judgment unconsciously.
- Signals: customer mood, environment, first screen, first sentence, or first image shifts conversion.
- Application: show the right proof, category cue, result image, or context before price.
- Verification question: "Truoc khi thay gia, khach da thay tin hieu nao de tin hay nghi ngo?"

### Anchoring And Adjustment
- Mechanism: the first number or standard becomes a mental anchor.
- Signals: customers call something expensive because the first anchor came from a cheaper substitute.
- Application: present premium/reference package first; compare against cost of doing nothing or inferior alternatives.
- Verification question: "Moc gia dau tien khach dang dung la moc nao?"

### Relativity
- Mechanism: people evaluate by comparison, not in isolation.
- Signals: customers ask "khac gi?", "sao ben kia re hon?", "goi nao phu hop?".
- Application: create a clean comparison set; use three packages or before-after alternatives.
- Verification question: "Dat canh option nao thi gia tri cua minh sang len?"

### Loss Aversion
- Mechanism: losses loom larger than equivalent gains.
- Signals: customers hesitate because of fear of regret, risk, wasted money, wrong choice.
- Application: emphasize cost of delay, guarantee, trial, risk reversal, mistake avoidance.
- Verification question: "Neu khong mua, khach dang mat gi ma ho chua thay ro?"

### Scarcity
- Mechanism: limited availability raises perceived value and urgency.
- Signals: customers act when slots, stock, bonus, or deadline is truly limited.
- Application: use honest scarcity only; make the constraint concrete.
- Verification question: "Gioi han nay co that va co lien quan den chat luong khong?"

### Herding
- Mechanism: when uncertain, people infer safety from what others do.
- Signals: customers ask who else uses it, popular package, best-seller, trend.
- Application: show category-specific adoption, popular choice, usage counts.
- Verification question: "Khach can thay nhom nguoi nao da chon truoc de yen tam?"

### Social Proof
- Mechanism: credible evidence from similar people reduces uncertainty.
- Signals: customers ask for reviews, case studies, screenshots, examples.
- Application: use testimonials with context, numbers, before-after, names where allowed.
- Verification question: "Bang chung nao khach tin hon loi minh noi?"

### Nudges And Choice Architecture
- Mechanism: decision design changes behavior without forcing choice.
- Signals: drop-off at forms, confusing package pages, customers fail to take obvious next step.
- Application: better defaults, clearer CTA, fewer fields, guided next step.
- Verification question: "Buoc nao co the lam de hon ma khong can thuyet phuc them?"

### Paradox Of Choice
- Mechanism: too many options increase cognitive load and delay.
- Signals: customers ask many questions then disappear; low conversion despite broad catalog.
- Application: reduce to recommended paths; use quiz/filters; label packages by use case.
- Verification question: "Minh dang bat khach tu phan loai qua nhieu khong?"

### Partitioning
- Mechanism: how value, effort, or cost is chunked changes perception.
- Signals: monthly cost feels high, onboarding feels heavy, package feels opaque.
- Application: break into daily cost, phases, milestones, modules, or checklists.
- Verification question: "Neu chia nho khac di, cam giac nang nhe thay doi the nao?"

### Pain Of Paying
- Mechanism: payment creates psychological pain, especially when salient or uncertain.
- Signals: hesitation right at checkout, discount requests, late invoice payment.
- Application: bundle, subscription, installment, clear ROI, payment timing, guarantee.
- Verification question: "Khoanh khac nao khach thay dau nhat khi tra tien?"

### Surprise And Delight
- Mechanism: unexpected positive moments create memory and word of mouth.
- Signals: customers share moments that exceeded expectations.
- Application: add small, relevant, unexpected value after commitment or delivery.
- Verification question: "Dieu nho nao lam khach nghi 'ben nay tinh te'?"

### Peak-End Rule
- Mechanism: people remember the emotional peak and the ending more than the average.
- Signals: overall service is good but memory depends on delivery, support, unboxing, final result.
- Application: design a strong peak moment and clean ending/follow-up.
- Verification question: "Diem dinh va diem ket cua trai nghiem khach la gi?"

### Habits
- Mechanism: repeated behavior follows cue, routine, reward.
- Signals: repeat purchase depends on reminders, replenishment cycles, familiar triggers.
- Application: build lifecycle reminders, subscriptions, saved preferences, rituals.
- Verification question: "Cue nao khien khach quay lai dung thoi diem?"

### Reciprocity
- Mechanism: useful value given first creates a felt obligation and trust.
- Signals: prospects respond better after receiving audit, checklist, sample, honest advice.
- Application: give diagnostic value before asking for purchase; avoid manipulative freebies.
- Verification question: "Gia tri nao cho truoc se giup khach ra quyet dinh tot hon?"

## Advisory Question Bank
Use these when diagnosing a case:

- Khach dang o giai doan nao: chu y, hieu, tin, can nhac rui ro, hanh dong, hay mua lai?
- Khach dang so sanh voi ai/cai gi?
- Gia dau tien khach thay la bao nhieu va den tu dau?
- No luc, rui ro, thoi gian, tien bac: cai nao lam khach dau nhat?
- Bang chung nao se lam khach tin nhung khong can minh noi qua nhieu?
- Neu khach khong mua hom nay, ho mat gi?
- Co qua nhieu lua chon khong? Lua chon nao nen la mac dinh?
- Khoanh khac nao trong hanh trinh dang lam khach roi di?
- Khach se ke lai dieu gi sau khi mua?
- Minh co the test thay doi nho nao trong 7 ngay?

## Consulting Style
- Be concrete and business-first.
- Do not overclaim certainty. Say "gia thuyet hanh vi" when the evidence is incomplete.
- Always connect concept to observable behavior and testable action.
- Prefer Vietnamese examples tied to real buying, hesitation, justification, memory, and word of mouth.
- When anh asks for strategy, include at least one verification question and one small experiment.
