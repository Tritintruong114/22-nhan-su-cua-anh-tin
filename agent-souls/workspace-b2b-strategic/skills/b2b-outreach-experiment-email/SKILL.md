# B2B Outreach Experiment Email

Use this skill whenever the user asks to draft, review, improve, or prepare a B2B outreach email for OpenClaw / FireGroup enterprise AI Agent sales.

## Core Standard

Every outreach email should follow this baseline:

1 persona -> 1 context -> 1 workflow hypothesis -> 1 outcome -> 1 CTA.

The email is an experiment to learn from the market, not just an asset to send. It should start from the account and the buyer's likely work, not from product capabilities.

## Before Writing

Identify and state briefly:

- Verified facts: public or user-provided facts directly relevant to the hypothesis.
- PIC / persona: their likely role in the workflow, not just title.
- Context / trigger hypothesis: why this account might care now.
- Workflow hypothesis: exactly one primary workflow to test.
- Customer value: the job outcome being tested.
- Unknowns: what is not known and could invalidate the angle.
- Adoption risks: control, trust, data access, security, sponsorship, timing.
- Confidence: high / medium / low.
- Learning signals: what reply, no reply, redirect, or objection would teach.

## Research Rule

Only use a company fact in the email if it connects directly to the hypothesis.

Pattern:

1 public fact -> 1 reasonable context inference -> 1 hypothesis to test.

Do not include facts just to prove research. Avoid reintroducing the company to its own employees.

Separate:

- Verified fact: confirmed from source or user input.
- Reasonable inference: market/operating implication, phrased carefully.
- Unknown: internal reality that must not be claimed.

Never turn a hypothesis into a fact. Do not write: "your team is spending too much time..." Prefer: "I wanted to test whether part of this workflow may still require..."

## Workflow Focus

Do not bundle many workflows into one hypothesis. Choose one primary workflow and at most one supporting example.

Examples by finance/accounting persona:

- Management accounting / FP&A: preparing periodic management reports -> reduce aggregation and checking time.
- Chief accountant / controller: reconciling unmatched items -> make discrepancies easier to identify and follow up.
- Finance operations: finding documents or information for case handling -> reduce time spent searching and asking across people/systems.
- CFO / financial control: tracking pending approvals or unresolved items -> improve visibility and control.

## Customer Value vs Control Conditions

Lead with work-language value:

- reduce time spent finding, aggregating, or checking information.
- make items that need review easier to see before close or approval.
- reduce missed follow-ups or unresolved exceptions.
- help the responsible team decide faster with clearer context.

Mention controls only after value, and only when relevant to the persona:

- sensitive information remains reviewed/approved by the team.
- scope, data access, and permissions are defined by the customer.
- start with a narrow pilot or evaluation scope.

Avoid leading with technical phrases like human-in-the-loop, audit trail, approval flow, or permissions unless the recipient is technical/governance-focused.

## CTA Rule

CTA should match relationship temperature. For cold outbound, ask for a small discovery conversation around the workflow, not a demo or broad AI discussion.

Good CTA pattern:

"I am not sure whether this is a current priority. If relevant, I would appreciate 20-30 minutes to understand whether [specific workflow] or [one adjacent check] is a part of the work worth reviewing. If you are not the right owner, could you point me to the right person?"

This creates two useful outcomes: direct conversation or buyer-map progress.

## Email Structure

Use this order:

1. Subject: persona + specific work, not product-led. AI Agent does not need to appear in the subject.
2. Greeting to the PIC.
3. Sender intro: concise, FireGroup/OpenClaw.
4. Verified context: 1-2 sentences tied directly to the workflow hypothesis.
5. Conditional hypothesis: one workflow, phrased as something to test.
6. Outcome: one concrete work result.
7. Control condition if needed: narrow scope, team retains review/approval/data-access control.
8. CTA: 20-30 minute discovery plus redirect-to-owner option.
9. Booking link when appropriate: https://calendar.app.google/BZwoXbtW71TQefHUA
10. Signature: use the user's approved FireGroup signature when drafting email for send.

## Subject Examples

- Trao đổi ngắn về việc chuẩn bị báo cáo quản trị tại [Company]
- Một câu hỏi về công việc đối chiếu số liệu tại [Company]
- Xin góc nhìn về [specific finance/accounting workflow] tại [Company]

## Pre-Send Checklist

Before sending or creating a draft, confirm:

- Which verified fact is relevant to the hypothesis?
- Which PIC/persona is addressed, and what workflow might they own?
- Which single workflow is being tested?
- Which pain is factual, and which pain is only a hypothesis?
- Which one outcome is worth 20 minutes of their time?
- If this person is not the owner, does the email make it easy to redirect?
- What should be learned from reply, no reply, redirect, or rejection?

## Safety

For outbound email actions, default to creating a Gmail draft with the approved FireGroup signature and wait for explicit user approval before sending. Do not send cold outreach directly unless the user clearly instructs that exact action.
