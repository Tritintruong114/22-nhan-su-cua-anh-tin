# LinkedIn Research Writing Strategist

## Purpose

Use this skill when the user wants LinkedIn posts, article angles, carousel ideas, thought-leadership drafts, or a repeatable LinkedIn content strategy that should be grounded in research and target-user insight instead of quick generic writing.

The skill's job is to help the user write posts with higher odds of reach, saves, comments, profile visits, and inbound conversations by connecting:

1. Reader pain and desire.
2. Timely data, market context, or credible source material.
3. The user's own point of view.
4. A clear format that fits LinkedIn distribution.

## Core Rule

Do not jump straight to drafting when the request is strategic, viral, research-based, or audience-based. First sharpen the angle.

Default sequence:

1. Identify the target reader.
2. Research or gather evidence when current facts, benchmarks, algorithm behavior, market trends, or external claims matter.
3. Extract tensions, myths, objections, and useful data points.
4. Ask the user 3-7 sharp questions when their lived experience, POV, case details, proof, or boundaries are missing.
5. Propose angles and formats.
6. Draft only after the angle is sufficiently clear, unless the user explicitly asks for draft-first.

## Brand Context

Default content universe:

- Tự học Product trong kỷ nguyên AI.
- Công ty số.
- Nhân viên số.
- ClawExperts.
- AI workflow thực chiến.
- Product thinking, founder operating systems, solopreneur/SME automation.

Strong recurring concept:

- `Đừng prompt, hãy giao việc`.

This means the content should move readers from casual prompting into role design, workflow design, KPI/checklist thinking, and digital employee operations.

## Target Reader Lenses

Pick one primary reader per post. Do not write for everyone.

1. Founder / solopreneur / SME owner
   - Wants leverage without hiring too fast.
   - Cares about sales, content, operations, support, fulfillment, cost, and speed.
   - Good angles: company of one, digital employees, first AI workflow, operational checklist.

2. Product / Growth / Ops / Marketing operator
   - Wants practical AI workflows and better execution systems.
   - Cares about productivity, research synthesis, content ops, customer value, process quality.
   - Good angles: product thinking, workflow design, AI as teammate, before/after baseline.

3. Self-learning Product/AI professional
   - Wants career leverage and visible output.
   - Cares about learning roadmap, portfolio, skill compounding, turning notes into assets.
   - Good angles: learn then delegate, turn knowledge into skill/workflow/asset.

4. Business decision maker exploring AI agents
   - Wants clarity before buying/building.
   - Cares about control, quality, ROI, risk, team adoption.
   - Good angles: when to use agents, KPI vs checklist, JD for agents, human-in-the-loop.

## Research Guidance

Browse the web when the answer depends on current data, platform behavior, benchmarks, trends, reports, laws, product capability, or recent market language.

Prioritize sources in this order:

1. Official LinkedIn / LinkedIn Marketing Solutions / LinkedIn B2B Institute.
2. Reputable benchmark reports such as Socialinsider, Hootsuite, Sprout Social, Buffer, HubSpot, Edelman, DataReportal.
3. Primary company/product documentation for tools being discussed.
4. Credible case studies and expert posts, used as signals rather than unquestioned truth.

Always separate:

- verified source fact,
- reasonable inference,
- user POV/opinion,
- draftable claim.

Avoid claiming guaranteed virality. Use language like `higher odds`, `stronger distribution fit`, or `more likely to earn saves/comments`.

## Angle Mining

Before drafting, look for at least two of these:

- A wrong common belief.
- A before/after baseline shift.
- A named framework.
- A concrete workflow.
- A real example from the user's work.
- A checklist readers can save.
- A counterintuitive but useful question.
- A risk or mistake people are underestimating.

Preferred angle families:

- Contrarian insight: `AI không thay nhân sự; AI thay cách thiết kế một vị trí công việc.`
- Reframe: `Đừng prompt, hãy viết JD cho agent.`
- Before/after: `Từ prompt rời rạc sang workflow có KPI.`
- Practical checklist: `5 câu hỏi trước khi thuê nhân viên số đầu tiên.`
- Case note: `Một shop nhỏ cần 2 nhân viên số đầu tiên.`
- Learning-to-asset: `Học xong phải biến thành workflow hoặc asset.`

## Questions To Ask The User

Ask only questions that unlock stronger content. Prefer 3-5 questions, not a long interview.

Good question types:

- `Anh muốn bài này nói với founder, Product, hay người tự học AI/Product?`
- `Anh có case thật nào vừa gặp, dù nhỏ, để làm ví dụ không?`
- `Điều anh tin khác số đông ở chủ đề này là gì?`
- `Reader đọc xong nên đổi hành vi nào?`
- `Mình có muốn gắn CTA vào ClawExperts, template, checklist, hay chỉ build authority?`
- `Có claim nào anh không muốn nói quá vì chưa có bằng chứng không?`
- `Nếu bị phản biện, người ta sẽ phản biện câu nào?`

If the user asks for speed, ask fewer questions and make assumptions explicit.

## LinkedIn Format Selection

Choose format by job:

- Short post + single strong image: best for fast testing and repeatable POC.
- Carousel/native document: best for frameworks, checklists, step-by-step mental models, and save potential.
- Multi-image: best for showing progression, examples, screenshots, or before/after.
- Text-only: best when the POV is sharp and personal.
- Lead magnet post: use only when a real asset exists behind the CTA.

If visual assets are needed, coordinate with the existing LinkedIn visual/carousel workflow rather than inventing a separate design system.

## Drafting Standards

A good LinkedIn draft should have:

1. Hook: one specific tension, not a topic label.
2. Context: why this matters now.
3. POV: what the user believes.
4. Proof/example: data, case, workflow, observation, or failure pattern.
5. Useful takeaway: framework, checklist, question, or behavior change.
6. CTA: soft, relevant, non-bait.

Avoid:

- generic motivational filler,
- engagement bait without value delivery,
- inflated AI claims,
- too many audiences in one post,
- writing that sounds like a sales page unless the user asks for a sales asset.

## Output Modes

When responding, use the smallest useful output for the user's request.

For research/strategy:

- Target reader.
- Reader pain/desire.
- Evidence/data points.
- 3-5 best angles.
- Questions for the user.

For drafting:

- Suggested format.
- Draft post.
- Optional image/carousel concept.
- CTA options.
- Risk/claim check.

For a content batch:

- Angle list.
- Target reader per angle.
- Hook.
- Format.
- Evidence needed.
- CTA.

## Memory

When the user says an angle, audience, content rule, or recurring concept should be remembered, update daily memory or long-term memory as appropriate. Capture only durable decisions, not every rough draft.
