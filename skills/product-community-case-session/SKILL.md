# Product Community Case Session Playbook

## Purpose
Use this playbook when helping Tri position, plan, or run events for his community around self-learning Product.

The community is not a generic beginner class and should not feel corporate. It is a warm, practical, slightly playful practitioner-led space where people learn Product by building, sharing real outputs, discussing real mistakes, and helping each other improve.

## Core Brand
Brand name:

> Cong dong tu hoc Product

Core positioning:

> A community for people who learn Product by self-learning, building real outputs, making mistakes, reflecting, and taking products toward the market.

Short positioning:

> Hoc Product bang cach build that, sai that, sua that, va dua san pham ra thi truong.

Tri's role:

> Practitioner-host: someone also self-learning, self-building, shipping real products/AI Agent outputs, sharing evidence and mistakes honestly, while creating a friendly space for others to learn and contribute.

Avoid making Tri sound like a distant lecturer or corporate trainer. Position him as an energetic, humorous, positive host who is a few steps ahead in some areas and is building alongside the community.

## Naming System
Use a warm commitment ladder:

1. Online top-of-funnel:
   - Tu hoc Product Meetup
   - Tu hoc Product: Buoi hoc chung #1

2. Offline deeper session:
   - Tu hoc Product Lab

3. Written/video case recaps:
   - Product Case Notes
   - Ghi chep tu hoc Product

Preferred online event title format:

> Tu hoc Product Meetup #1: [Specific Topic]

Alternative playful title:

> Tu hoc Product: Buoi hoc chung #1 - [Specific Topic]

Avoid using overly formal titles like Product Case Session for first-touch online events. They may create unnecessary friction for people who identify as self-learners.

## Event Ladder
Use two event types together:

1. Online Meetup / Buoi hoc chung
   - Light, accessible, top-of-funnel.
   - Goal: attract interested people, clarify positioning, create trust, and invite people to the offline lab.
   - Format: 45-60 minutes, free, structured but warm.
   - Tone: vui ve, gan gui, nang dong, khong lecture.

2. Offline Product Lab
   - Curated, higher-trust, limited seats.
   - Goal: create core community, get deeper discussion, collect feedback, and develop member ownership.
   - Format: 2 hours, invite-only or low-ticket/symbolic ticket at first.

Growth loop:

Real output -> online meetup -> interested people apply/register -> offline lab -> discussion and member stories -> recap content -> new people discover community -> next session.

## Community Norms
Every session should create psychological safety early.

Core norm:

> Chung ta o day ai cung dang hoc, ai cung co lan sai, co lan fail, co lan build xong khong ai dung. Do la chuyen rat binh thuong khi lam san pham. Dieu quan trong la minh co noi that, hoc that, gop y chan thanh, va cung nhau tot len hay khong.

Session rules:
- No flexing inflated success.
- No aggressive pitching.
- Share honestly, including mistakes and unclear parts.
- Give sincere, specific, constructive feedback.
- Receive feedback with curiosity, not defensiveness.
- Protect the tone: friendly, practical, positive, not judgmental.

## Ice-Breaking Structure
Use this at the start of online and offline sessions.

Duration: 5-10 minutes.

Host opening example:

> Truoc khi vao noi dung, minh muon noi truoc mot dieu: day khong phai lecture, cung khong phai san khau de ai phai to ra minh qua gioi. Day la mot buoi hoc chung. Ai o day chac cung tung co y tuong bi bo do, build thu gi do ma khong ai dung, hoac dung AI nhung van bi roi. Minh cung vay. Nen hom nay minh cu noi that, hoi that, gop y that, va giu vibe vui ve voi nhau.

Quick ice-break prompts:
- Ban dang hoc/build gi gan day?
- Mot thu ban tung build/thich build nhung bi ket la gi?
- Mot viec AI dang giup ban lam nhanh hon la gi?
- Mot cau hoi ve Product/AI ban dang muon go la gi?

For online sessions, use chat-first ice-breaking:

> Moi nguoi tha vao chat 1 dong: ten + dang build/hoc gi + mot cau hoi dang ket.

Then Tri reads 3-5 responses aloud and connects them to the session topic.

## Online Meetup Structure
Use this for first-touch online events.

Duration: 45-60 minutes.

Suggested title:

> Tu hoc Product Meetup #1: Cach AI thay doi quy trinh phat trien san pham 2026

Suggested description:

> Mot buoi hoc chung vui ve cho nhung nguoi dang tu hoc Product, dang build hoac muon build san pham, cung nhin lai cach AI dang thay doi research, spec, prototype, launch va iterate.

Agenda:

1. Warm Opening + Ice-break, 5-10 minutes
   - What this community is about.
   - Set the shared-failure norm.
   - Invite people to introduce themselves in chat.
   - Emphasize: not lecture, not success theater, not tool-listing.

2. Main Sharing, 20-25 minutes
   - Topic context.
   - What changed in Product workflow.
   - Practical examples from AI/Product work.

3. Mini Case / Honest Note, 10 minutes
   - What was built or tested.
   - What worked.
   - What did not work or remains unclear.
   - What to test next.

4. Q&A / Group Reflection, 10-15 minutes
   - Prioritize questions about self-learning Product, AI workflows, early traction, building consistently, and product mistakes.

5. Community CTA, 5 minutes
   - Invite people who are building or learning Product seriously to join the offline lab.
   - Ask who wants to share a mini case in the future.

Online CTA:

> Neu ban dang tu hoc Product va muon co mot noi de hoc bang case that, output that, sai that va sua that, hay tham gia cung minh trong cac buoi meetup/lab tiep theo.

## Offline Product Lab Structure
Duration: 2 hours.
Audience: 10-20 selected participants.
Pricing: first session free/invite-only or symbolic ticket 99k-199k; later increase after trust and format quality are proven.

Agenda:

1. Warm Opening + Ice-break, 10-15 minutes
   - Theme of the day.
   - Community rule: honest case notes, no inflated success claims, no aggressive pitching.
   - Shared-failure framing.

2. Main Case by Tri, 30-35 minutes
   - Context and problem.
   - Product/output demo.
   - Early traction or evidence.
   - Decisions and trade-offs.
   - Mistakes, unclear signals, and lessons.
   - Next hypothesis.

3. Member Mini Case, 15-20 minutes
   - One selected member shares what they are building.
   - Structure: what I am building, who it is for, current signal, where I am stuck, feedback I want.

4. Group Discussion, 30 minutes
   - What is clear?
   - What is unclear?
   - What should be tested next?
   - What feedback is specific and useful?

5. Takeaway Round, 10-15 minutes
   - Each participant shares one takeaway and one action for the next 7 days.

6. Closing, 5-10 minutes
   - Summarize 3 insights.
   - Announce next topic.
   - Invite next member case.

## Framing Early Traction
If the product has only a few paid users, do not frame it as a success story. Frame it as an early traction case.

Good framing:

> This is not a big success story yet. It is a small but real signal. The useful question is what this signal proves, what it does not prove, and what should be tested next.

Use language like:
- early traction
- organic signal
- product evidence
- case notes
- learning from the market
- mistakes and next experiments

Avoid:
- proven product
- successful launch
- growth case study
- strong PMF

## Monthly Operating Rhythm
Each month should produce:
- 1 online meetup or offline lab
- 1 written recap
- 3-5 short clips or posts
- 1 member/customer/product insight
- 1 candidate for next member case

Every 3 months, review:
- number of repeat participants
- number of members building/sharing
- quality of discussion
- new product/user insights generated
- whether the community is contributing to Tri's work, brand, and income goals

## Strategic Fit Check
Before adding a new event or activity, ask:

1. Does this strengthen Tri's AI Agent/Product positioning?
2. Does it create useful content or case notes?
3. Does it attract serious builders while staying welcoming to self-learners?
4. Does it create feedback for a product/output?
5. Does it support long-term goals: better career opportunities, higher income, assets, and semi-passive income?

If the answer is mostly no, simplify or skip the activity.
